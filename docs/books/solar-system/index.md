# The solar system

*The Sun holds nearly all the mass of its family, but the rest is far more varied: scorched Mercury and cloudy Venus,
our own Earth and Moon, red Mars, a belt of rubble, four giant planets with dozens of moons each, and icy worlds and
comets out to where the Sun's pull fades away. This book visits them all, one by one, and then compares them: what
they are made of, how they got their air and their magnetism, and where there might be water and life. Mars is never
far away, because every world tells us something about it.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter uses the ones before it. [Orbits](../orbits/index.md) explains how the planets move, and
  [Light and telescopes](../light-and-telescopes/index.md) how they are observed, but neither is needed.
- **Every level.** Each chapter starts gently. The middle sections go further, "Working it out" does the numbers, and
  the last section is for readers who want the full story.
- **The physics is on another site.** Where an idea is taught step by step on
  [physics.akadian.ca](https://physics.akadian.ca/), the chapter links to the lesson instead of repeating it.
- **Mars beside the Earth.** Wherever the numbers are compared, Mars comes first.
- **Mars has its own book.** This book compares Mars with its neighbours; the next book, *Mars*, tells its full story.
- **The history is in another book.** For the full story of who found what, follow the links to the
  [history of astronomy](../history/index.md).
