# Orbits

*Everything in the sky is falling. The Moon falls round the Earth, the Earth and Mars fall round the Sun, and the Sun
falls round the middle of the Milky Way. This book is about those falls that never reach the ground. It starts with
Newton's cannon and Kepler's laws, goes on to gravity, tides and the pulls of many bodies on one another, and then to
the rockets, transfer orbits and gravity assists that carry spacecraft between the planets. It ends at Mars, with the
orbiters that circle it, the fate of its moon Phobos, and the slow changes in Mars's own orbit and tilt.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter uses the ones before it. The first book, [The night sky](../night-sky/index.md), helps
  with where the planets appear, and [Light and telescopes](../light-and-telescopes/index.md) with how they are
  observed, but neither is needed.
- **Every level.** Each chapter starts gently. The middle sections go further, "Working it out" does the numbers, and
  the last section is for readers who want the full story.
- **The physics is on another site.** Where an idea is taught step by step on
  [physics.akadian.ca](https://physics.akadian.ca/), the chapter links to the lesson instead of repeating it.
- **Mars beside the Earth.** Wherever the numbers are compared, Mars comes first.
- **The history is in another book.** For the full story of who found what, follow the links to the
  [history of astronomy](../history/index.md).
